What is Hangeul?

The Korean alphabet, Hangul (called 조선글, joseongeul, in North Korea) was born in the 15th century thanks to the determination of King Sejong the Great, the fourth king of the Joseon Dynasty, to educate the population.

Previously, the Korean language used the Chinese writing system based on hanja, which was very difficult to learn, especially for the poorest people who lacked access to education. To solve this problem, King Sejong decided to introduce a new phonetic writing system, commissioning its invention to a group of scholars and erudites.

Hunminjeongeum (훈민정음) was the first book to promulgate the Korean alphabet, Hangul, and its title means "the correct sounds for the instruction of the people".

Initially, the upper classes opposed its introduction, considering it a writing method for the uneducated, and thus, the Korean alphabet, Hangul, had to struggle to gain a place in common use. At first, it was used primarily by the poor and women. However, it was later revived as an attempt to separate itself from the Chinese sphere of influence and seen as a national symbol.

The importance of this invention is deeply felt by the Korean people, who still celebrate its anniversary today. October 9th, designated a national holiday, is Hangul Day (한글날, hangeulnal), during which many businesses and schools remain closed.
